01

The 60-Second Summary

The settled fact: a single photograph cannot measure absolute size — scale is mathematically unobservable from one image, so every "metric" monocular system imports its units from somewhere else: camera intrinsics, training-set size priors, an IMU, a depth sensor, or a known reference object. What has genuinely changed is that foundation depth models (Metric3D, ZoeDepth, UniDepth, Depth Pro, MoGe-2) now package remarkably good statistical scale priors, so metric output works well where the test scene resembles the training distribution. The contested question is not whether these models produce metric numbers — it is whether a learned prior that collapses from ~90% to ~30% accuracy off-distribution should be called "measurement" at all. Practitioners resolve the tension operationally: nobody who needs the number ships raw model scale; production systems anchor it (AR to inertial tracking, robotics to kinematics or stereo, mapping to ground control), and the best recent models (MoGe-2, Depth Pro) themselves decouple scale-free geometry from a separate scale estimate. The economically-fueled "camera-only" narrative and the 170-year engineering record point the same way: the winning designs delegate units to an explicit anchor rather than pretending the image contains them.

02

Five Key Findings, Ranked by Reliability

Absolute scale is always externally referenced — the reference has moved from hardware into data, but it never disappeared
1
Reliability: High
9/10

Single-view metrology has required an in-scene metric reference since it was formalised (Criminisi, Reid & Zisserman, ICCV 1999); photogrammetry has required surveyed ground control for over a century; monocular SLAM adopted IMU fusion specifically because "IMUs add metric scale to the otherwise scale-invariant results" (Visual-Inertial ORB-SLAM, 2016). Modern metric depth models do not escape this: Metric3D's canonical-camera transform is built on known focal length (its released code falls back to guessing among default focal lengths when intrinsics are absent), and MiDaS deliberately chose scale-and-shift-invariant training because metric supervision does not mix across datasets. The learned prior is the newest form of the reference, not its elimination.

Zero-shot metric accuracy is a domain lottery: the same model swings from near-perfect to unusable across test sets
2
Reliability: High
8/10

Under Depth Pro's controlled zero-shot metric protocol (ICLR 2025) on unseen datasets, δ₁ accuracy for leading "metric" models spans enormous ranges — Metric3D v2 roughly 30→88, UniDepth roughly 17→96, ZoeDepth roughly 8→86 — and the paper documents that several systems quietly violate zero-shot conditions (separate indoor/outdoor metric heads; per-domain test-time crop selection). Headline δ₁≈0.97 numbers on NYUv2/KITTI are in-domain artifacts, not general capability.

3

8/10

Relative (scale-invariant) geometry generalises zero-shot; absolute scale regresses toward the training-domain prior. MoGe-2's ablations (Table 4) show that predicting affine-invariant geometry plus a separate low-dimensional global scale head beats direct metric regression — the paper attributes the failure of entangled designs to focal-distance ambiguity and gradient interference. Field reports match: MoGe-2's far-field failure (Arc de Triomphe predicted 18.8 m vs actual 44.8 m, per MetricScenes) was a scale failure on intact shape, fixed by adding new training data, not new geometry.

4
Reliability: Med-High
7/10

AR frameworks (ARKit/ARCore) recover scale from visual-inertial odometry, where the IMU makes scale observable; robotics manipulation work reports SOTA metric depth failing to generalise to real grasping and adds kinematic anchors (KineDepth: robot-as-measuring-stick, 22% depth-error reduction); drone mapping still mandates ground control points. Camera-only purism has repeatedly reverted: Tesla reintroduced radar (HW4, 2023); Apple added LiDAR (2020).

03

The Hidden Connection

On the surface, the lenses disagree about whether learned metric depth is progress (Academic) or a memorisation trick (Skeptic), and whether the camera-only push will win (Economist) or revert to fusion (Historian).

But line up what each lens found works, and the same design appears at every layer of the stack: MiDaS and MoGe-2 architecturally split scale-free geometry from a scalar scale estimate; deployed AR splits SLAM geometry from IMU-observed scale; photogrammetry split image geometry from a surveyed baseline for 170 years; and the money splits the same way — shape is commoditised and given away free, while scale is where liability, hardware cost, and product value concentrate.

Every generation of monocular 3D independently rediscovers the same architecture: make geometry scale-free and delegate the units to an explicit anchor. Progress is real, but it is progress in the quality and availability of anchors — not in removing the need for one.

The assumption this briefing rests on (and the missing 6th lens)

All five lenses implicitly assumed the question is "how accurate is monocular metric scale?" None asked the metrologist's question: how accurate does it need to be, per application, and who certifies the claim? A furniture-in-room AR preview may tolerate 10% error; a grasp planner millimetres; an autonomy safety case has formal requirements. Without a requirements-and-certification lens, "works" and "fails" are unfalsifiable vibes — and today's benchmarks, built exclusively from prior-conforming scenes, are structurally unable to catch prior failures (a dollhouse kitchen confidently measured as a real kitchen).

This is the omission that could invert the findings: if most commercial applications only require prior-level accuracy, the Skeptic's critique is scientifically right and commercially irrelevant — the "frozen tape measure" is good enough for the money that funds it.

04

What To Actually Do Differently

For a researcher submitting a paper on metric scale in monocular reconstruction — specific positioning and evaluation moves, drawn from where the lenses converge.

01
Frame contributions as anchor-delegation and anchor-correction, never as "solving" metric scale.

Finding 1 is the consensus of the field's own history and its best architectures; reviewers steeped in Criminisi/MiDaS/MoGe-2 will reward the honest frame and punish a "we make monocular 3D metric" claim.

02
Report scale error decomposed from shape error — it is the experiment the literature is missing.

The Academic lens found that almost no metric-depth paper separates a global log-scale residual from scale-invariant shape residual, so the field cannot say whether "metric" gains are geometry or prior-guessing. A paper that publishes this decomposition (per-domain, in-distribution vs zero-shot) fills a named gap.

03
State your intrinsics policy explicitly, and test self-estimated vs supplied focal length.

Metric claims are conditional on intrinsics handling (Finding 1). Practitioner evidence says supplying ground-truth FOV can hurt models trained on self-estimated intrinsics — if you observe this, report it; it is a publishable calibration finding, not a bug to hide.

04
Never quote in-domain metric numbers as general capability; adopt a Depth-Pro-style cross-domain protocol.

Finding 2 shows single-benchmark metric numbers are the field's least trustworthy statistic. Report per-source spreads and disclose any per-domain heads, crops, or tuning — before a reviewer finds them.

05
Pre-empt the "category-size memoriser" objection in your limitations.

Acknowledge that prior-conforming benchmarks cannot detect prior failures (the dollhouse test), and where feasible include one prior-violating probe — unusual object sizes, atypical focal lengths — even as a qualitative figure.

06
Cite the delegation lineage as your intellectual scaffolding.

Criminisi's reference-height metrology → photogrammetric ground control → VIO scale observability → MiDaS's deliberate scale-invariance → MoGe-2's decoupled scale head. Positioning your method as the object-level continuation of a 170-year design pattern is stronger than claiming novelty against it.

04b

What's Safe to Assert

✓ Safe — verified against primary source

  • Metric scale from a single image is ill-posed; systems recover it via intrinsics + learned priors or external anchors (Metric3D TPAMI; Criminisi 1999).
  • Zero-shot metric accuracy varies drastically across domains, and some "zero-shot" systems use per-domain heads or crops (Depth Pro, ICLR 2025).
  • MiDaS deliberately trained scale-and-shift-invariant to enable cross-dataset mixing (Ranftl et al., TPAMI 2020).
  • Monocular SLAM adopted inertial fusion specifically to obtain metric scale (Mur-Artal & Tardós, 2016; ORB-SLAM3 2020).

⚠ Say with a caveat

  • Specific δ₁ ranges per model — attribute to Depth Pro's protocol and note protocol sensitivity.
  • MoGe-2's decoupled-scale superiority — cite the ablation table, note it is one model family's evidence.
  • GitHub-issue failure cases (2-5× scale errors) — real but anecdotal; use as motivation, not measurement.
  • "Supplying GT intrinsics can hurt" — practitioner-observed and mechanism-plausible; verify on your own stack before asserting.

✕ Don't assert

  • Market forecasts ($1.2T spatial computing) or the "94% AR conversion lift" as facts.
  • That any current model achieves reliable zero-shot metric scale "without priors".
  • Precise valuations/savings figures from secondary press (Tesla $114/vehicle; World Labs $5B talks) without "reportedly".
  • That benchmark agreement proves measurement rather than prior-matching — no current benchmark can distinguish them.
05

The Frontier Question

Can any evaluation distinguish measured scale from memorised scale?

Every current benchmark is built from prior-conforming scenes, so a model that memorises category sizes and one that genuinely infers scale are indistinguishable on paper. A benchmark of deliberately prior-violating scenes — miniatures, oversized props, unusual optics, decomposed into global-scale vs shape residuals — would settle the Academic-vs-Skeptic contradiction, expose how much of the current metric wave is a frozen tape measure, and define what "metric" is allowed to mean on a spec sheet. No lens addressed it because no one profits from building it: vendors would only lose headline numbers, and academics lack the incentive to attack the benchmarks their results depend on.
